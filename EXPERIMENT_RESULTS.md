# Benchmark Results and Interpretation

## Experimental setup

The included benchmark is generated by `python benchmark.py`. The checked-in sample results use:

- 5 workload seeds: 42, 43, 44, 45, and 46
- 4 synthetic processes with 400 references each (1,600 references per completed run)
- 16 physical frames
- FIFO, LRU, Clock, and Optimal page replacement
- TLB enabled and disabled
- Adaptive management enabled and disabled

This yields 80 runs. Workload seeds and configuration are held constant when paired settings are compared. The workloads are synthetic locality-based reference streams, not traces collected from macOS or Windows.

## Page replacement and adaptive control

The table below reports the mean page-fault count across the five seeds. TLB is enabled in these comparisons; the TLB does not affect which pages fault, but it does affect page-table walk overhead.

| Policy | Adaptive control | Mean page faults | Mean page-fault rate | Std. dev. of faults | Mean suspensions per run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| FIFO | Off | 1,046.6 | 65.4% | 10.3 | 0.0 |
| FIFO | On | 676.4 | 42.3% | 19.6 | 32.6 |
| LRU | Off | 1,029.0 | 64.3% | 11.8 | 0.0 |
| LRU | On | 666.6 | 41.7% | 25.1 | 33.4 |
| Clock | Off | 1,045.6 | 65.3% | 10.4 | 0.0 |
| Clock | On | 677.2 | 42.3% | 12.2 | 33.0 |
| Optimal* | Off | 713.6 | 44.6% | 6.9 | 0.0 |
| Optimal* | On | 610.8 | 38.2% | 21.6 | 31.4 |

For these five generated workloads, adaptive control coincides with lower mean page-fault counts: a reduction of about 35.4% for FIFO, 35.2% for LRU, 35.2% for Clock, and 14.4% for Optimal compared with the corresponding non-adaptive runs. The intervention includes redistribution of frames and temporarily suspending a process, so these values measure the combined policy rather than frame redistribution in isolation.

`Optimal*` has access to the future references in the generated sequence. It is an oracle benchmark used for comparison, not a realistic online replacement policy.

## TLB comparison

Across the 40 TLB-on and 40 TLB-off runs, mean page-table access counts per run were:

| Setting | Mean page-table accesses per run |
|---|---:|
| TLB enabled | 2,017.5 |
| TLB disabled | 3,028.1 |

That is approximately 33.4% fewer simulated page-table accesses with the TLB enabled across this experiment set. The mean TLB hit rate across TLB-on runs was approximately 31.6%. Paired runs had identical page-fault counts, as expected: the TLB caches translations but does not change whether a virtual page is resident.

## Reading the results correctly

- These are measured outputs from this simulator's synthetic workloads, with five seeds. They are useful for demonstrating and debugging the mechanisms, not for making universal claims about real operating systems.
- “CPU utilization” is an illustrative proxy calculated from page-fault overhead and memory pressure. It is not measured CPU utilization of the Mac or PC.
- “Memory pressure” and “predicted pressure” are derived from estimated working-set demand and frame capacity; they are not host operating-system memory-pressure signals.
- Temporary suspension is simulated by delaying a process's next memory references. The application does not suspend real processes.
- More robust research would add several workload types, many more seeds, confidence intervals, configurable detector thresholds, and ablation experiments that isolate frame redistribution from process suspension.
